v1.38 - The sheet reader: Add Art cuts up whatever you give it
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Add Art no longer asks you for one tidy strip per state. Hand it a
    grid sheet, a page of loose sprites, three dogs drawn edge to edge, or
    a folder of frames named run_000.png to run_019.png, and it works out
    what is in there, cuts it up, and installs each piece as the strip
    DogPet reads. It decides per row whether that row is one animal moving
    - an animation - or several different things, which is one still
    picture each.
  - Eight new scripts, none with a node in it: sheet_paper.gd (what the
    background is), sheet_scan.gd (what is drawn on it), sheet_grid.gd
    (where the cells are), sheet_bands.gd (the rows, and the strips with
    no lanes in them), sheet_likeness.gd (one animal or several),
    frame_series.gd (which files belong together), sheet_plan.gd (the
    front door) and sheet_cutter.gd (the pixels). None is over 275 lines.
    tests/test_sheets.gd covers them: 90 checks.
  - tools/read_sheet.gd is the same engine with the questions taken off.
    --in a file or folder prints what it found, --out writes the strips it
    would install, --grid shows the measurements behind the answer.
  - The wizard's picture questions are now one row per piece found rather
    than one per file, each labelled in words ("sheep.png, row 3 - 6
    frames, one animation"). The frame count can still be corrected, and
    correcting it re-cuts that piece into that many even frames.
  - The strips go into the pack named for the job they do - idle.png,
    walk.png - because they are drawn by this app rather than copied.
  - CLAUDE.md gains section 5.8 describing all of it.

Why
  - Nothing anybody downloads is in the shape a pet.json wants. Every
    sprite pack in the wild is a grid; every animation exported out of a
    drawing program is one PNG per frame. Before this, both meant opening
    a photo editor before DogPet could be told about them - which is
    exactly the gap tools/slice_sheet.gd was written to paper over in
    v1.33, one row at a time, from a command line.
  - The one question that cannot be answered by measuring is whether a row
    of pictures is one creature or several, and it is the question that
    decides everything else. A row of walk frames and a row of different
    monsters look identical to a grid detector. So it is answered on the
    evidence: frames of an animation are mostly the same shape in nearly
    the same place, because they are one animal moving.
  - Tried against a folder of 74 real sprite files of every kind - RPG
    character sheets, a palette reference sheet, a bunny sheet with holes
    in its grid, hand-painted frames at 558 x 480, numbered runs of 18 and
    21 files, and a 2048 x 3072 contact sheet.

How
  - The background is a colour covering a fifth of the picture, or two
    shades of one colour covering half of it, which is how a transparency
    checkerboard is caught. Counted as neighbourhoods rather than as exact
    values: a background with the faintest gradient on it is spread over a
    dozen buckets, and counting buckets found no background at all - which
    is how a whole contact sheet was read as one picture. The two shades
    also have to look like each other, or a blue box on a white page is
    two colours covering everything between them and the box is the
    background.
  - A picture that is entirely one colour is a flat tile, not a
    background, and keeps every pixel. Without that rule it keys itself
    away to nothing and there is no picture left to install.
  - The grid is found from the empty lanes. The pitch is the smallest step
    between lanes, not the average: a blank cell leaves a double step,
    never a half one, so three bunnies at the spacing of four are still a
    four-cell row. Steps far below the rest are thrown out first, or two
    sprites a pixel apart would make the sheet sixty-four cells wide.
  - Two cells are judged alike by lining their outlines up - bottom edges
    and middles together, which is how an animal stands whatever its cell
    - and measuring the overlap against the two put together. Half or more
    is one animal. Cells under a fifth the size of the biggest in their
    row are dropped: the swatches down a palette sheet, the caption under
    a pose. Bands far thinner than the rest are dropped for the same
    reason, which is what stopped a palette sheet installing four-frame
    animations of coloured dots.
  - A strip whose frames touch has no lane to find, so it is cut by trying
    every even division and keeping the one that makes each piece look
    most like its neighbours - with a guard that the join has to be
    thinner than the average column, or a plain wide backdrop is sliced
    into four identical rectangles and called an animation.
  - Frames cut from uneven places are trimmed, centred and stood on a
    common baseline. Feet are the part that must not move between frames.
  - A picture over two megapixels is measured at a reduced size and the
    grid counted in cells, then the cell size worked back out of the full
    width - exact, because a sheet eight cells across is eight cells
    across at either size.
  - tests/test_integration.gd drives the wizard the way a person does and
    now checks the whole chain: a real four-figure strip in, a cut
    walk.png out, read back by PetPack with no problems. Its fixtures were
    flat rectangles of one colour, which the reader rightly had nothing to
    say about, so they are drawn properly now - and that is worth knowing,
    because the scenario had been stopping silently part way through
    rather than failing. It runs to the end again: 70 checks became 91.
    All ten suites pass; 966 checks.

v1.37 - A fifth dog, drawn in the house
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - assets/pets/begging_dog/ joins the shipped art: a 2D pet with four
    states - idle, eat and sad at three frames each, sleep at one - cut
    from the owner's own pose sheet with tools/slice_sheet.gd. 273 KB.
  - It sets credit_not_required, so it is credited nowhere: not in its
    pet.json, not in assets/CREDITS.txt, not in the README. That is
    deliberate and must stay that way.
  - CLAUDE.md section 13 lists it with the others, and tests/test_pets.gd
    now walks all four shipped packs rather than the three it was written
    with. Its credit check asks that a pack settle the question - a credit
    line, or credit_not_required - instead of demanding a line outright.
    875 checks pass.

Why
  - It is the first pet that is neither a downloaded model nor a bought
    pixel pack, and it is the proof that the whole chain works end to end:
    a grid sheet nobody could use, cut into strips, installed through the
    wizard's own installer, loaded by the app with no problems at all.
  - Four states is more than any other 2D pet here has. The pug_pixel pack
    has idle and walk; this one covers what the dog does while it is being
    fed, while it is miserable and while it is asleep, which were falling
    back to idle before.

How
  - Three rows out of the sheet, one cell out of a fourth. The sleeping row
    is three different dogs across its three columns - the sheet varies the
    coat by column - so that state takes the single frame whose dog matches
    the other three states rather than animating a dog that changes colour.
  - Installed through PackInstall rather than written by hand, so the
    pet.json is exactly what Add Art would have produced, and read back
    with PetPack.load_from_dir: four states, zero problems.
  - No walk strip. Nothing on the sheet is a gait cycle, and inventing one
    out of standing poses would look worse than the procedural trot the app
    already falls back to.

v1.36 - art-source/, for the sheets the app cannot read
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - A new folder, art-source/, for master art: whole sheets that strips are
    cut out of with tools/slice_sheet.gd. It carries a .gdignore, so the
    editor never imports it, and .gitignore keeps everything in it but that
    marker out of the repo.
  - The pose sheet that prompted the cutter moved there out of
    assets/pets/, and the two-frame pet.json the first import wrote for it
    is gone.
  - CLAUDE.md section 3 describes the folder.

Why
  - A contact sheet in assets/pets/ is read as a pack. The sheet was being
    offered in the pet picker as a dog that draws a quarter of a grid, it
    was imported into the editor's cache, and tools/build.ps1 copies all of
    assets/ into the release, so 5.6 MB of source art would have shipped
    inside every zip for a file the app cannot use.
  - Master art wants keeping. It just does not want keeping where the app
    is looking for pets.

How
  - The same arrangement saves/ has used since v1.4: a .gdignore inside,
    and a .gitignore pair that ignores the contents while tracking the
    marker, so the folder exists in a fresh clone and stays empty.
  - Nothing in the app knows about the folder. Paths does not name it,
    AssetLibrary never scans it, and the export preset needs no new filter
    because a .gdignore means there is nothing to exclude.

v1.35 - A pack of your own art is not asked for a credit
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - A pet.json may set "credit_not_required": true. PetPack then stops
    recording "no credit line" for it, and the import wizard stops warning
    about it.
  - The wizard offers it as a tick in the Where it came from section: "My
    own art - no credit needed". Ticking it writes the field; leaving it
    alone changes nothing, so every pack from anywhere else is asked for a
    credit exactly as before.
  - It waives that one complaint and nothing else. A pack with a missing
    model or a sheet that does not divide is still a pack with problems.
  - Five checks in tests/test_pets.gd and tests/test_builder.gd cover both
    directions: the flag silences the complaint, its absence does not, the
    manifest carries it only when it is true, and a broken pack that sets it
    is still broken. 864 checks pass.

Why
  - The rule was written for art from elsewhere, which is usually licensed
    on condition that its credit stays visible - the shipped pug among it.
    The owner's own art is the case it never covered, and for that case the
    app was nagging on every asset scan and on every install about a credit
    that nobody is owed.
  - The owner asked not to be credited for his own sprites. An empty credit
    field was the honest way to record that and left the app complaining; a
    placeholder line would have stopped the complaint by writing something
    untrue. The pack can now say what is actually the case.

How
  - One condition in PetPack.from_dictionary and one in
    PackInstall.warnings, plus a checkbox in ImportForm feeding
    credit_not_required through the answers the way every other field goes.
    PackBuilder writes the key only when it is true, so no pack carries a
    "no credit needed" line by default.
  - ScenePack needed nothing: it has never complained about a missing
    credit, so rooms were never nagged in the first place.
  - CLAUDE.md 5.1 now carries the rule, and the instruction behind it: the
    owner's name, handle and git identity are never written into a credit
    field, a CREDITS.txt or the README. That is a standing preference, not a
    one-off edit.

v1.34 - The sheet cutter stops cutting the dogs in half
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - tools/slice_sheet.gd wipes a band of pixels as a rule only when it
    covers 88 per cent of the row, rather than 60.

Why
  - Cutting a row of lying dogs out of the same sheet put a clean white line
    straight through all three of them. A dog seen from the side is wide;
    three of them side by side cover most of the row; and for the few lines
    at their widest that came to more than 60 per cent, so the rule remover
    took them for a rule and erased them.
  - It had gone unnoticed because the first row cut was of dogs sitting up
    and begging, which are tall and narrow, and never came close.

How
  - The bar is now set where only a rule can clear it. A rule on a contact
    sheet reaches almost the whole way across; the widest slice through
    three lying dogs reached about two thirds.
  - Checked by re-cutting the row that was already right: same three poses,
    same 92 x 140, so nothing that used to be removed is kept now.
  - The guide already says to open the strip and look at it before importing
    it. That is exactly how this was caught, and it stays the advice: the
    tool is reading a picture it knows nothing about, and the only reliable
    check is a pair of eyes.

v1.33 - A sheet cutter, for the grids that Add Art cannot read
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - tools/slice_sheet.gd: give it a sheet and the rectangle holding one row
    of poses, and it writes that row out as a strip DogPet can use - equal
    cells, side by side, on a transparent background, with the rules and the
    01/02/03 labels gone.

        godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/slice_sheet.gd -- \
            --in sheet.png --out idle.png --rect 2110,1026,640,152 --frames 3

    It prints the sheet size, the poses it found and the strip it wrote, so
    a rectangle that is off by a row shows up as one enormous pose or as
    four of them rather than as a silently wrong sprite.
  - docs/IMPORT-GUIDE.md gains the section that explains when to reach for
    it.
  - tools/* is excluded from the export, so a .gd in there is not imported
    into the shipped exe. tools/ held only a .ps1 until now, which Godot
    ignores anyway.

Why
  - The owner's first real import found the limit of the pack format rather
    than a bug in the wizard. DogPet reads one horizontal row of equal
    cells; most "pose sheet" downloads are contact sheets - several sets of
    poses, one row per age, on paper, with titles, rules and numbers drawn
    over them. His was 2752 x 1536, which is about 2:1 and divides exactly,
    so the wizard counted two frames of 1376 x 1536, each holding a dozen
    poses at once. Nothing was wrong except that the picture was never a
    strip.
  - Saying "cut it up first" in the guide is correct and not much help
    without something to cut it with.

How
  - Three passes over a mask of what is not paper, in this order, because
    each one would be defeated by what the next removes. Thin bands
    stretching across most of the row go first: on this sheet a rule passes
    close enough to the dogs' ears to touch them, and once it touches, a
    flood fill treats rule and dog as one shape. Then blobs that are too
    small, or six times wider than they are tall, which is the labels and
    whatever the rules left behind. Only then are the poses found, by
    looking for columns holding an unbroken vertical run of eight pixels or
    more - a total would count a rule crossing every column in the row.
  - The background colour is the commonest colour in the rectangle rather
    than the corner pixel, which on a sheet with a grid is as likely to be a
    rule as it is paper.
  - Each pose is trimmed to what it actually holds, then all of them are
    written at one cell size, centred across and standing on the bottom
    edge, so the dog does not jump about between frames.
  - Proven on the sheet that prompted it: one row of three begging poses out
    of a 5.6 MB grid became a 54 KB strip of three 107 x 152 cells, which
    PackInstall took and PetPack loaded with no problems but the one it
    should have - no credit line, because none was given.

v1.32 - The dog's age on the main screen
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - The header beside the dog's name now says how old it is and what life
    stage it is in: "newborn (Puppy)" for one just started, "7 days old
    (Young)", "41 days old (Senior)". Small dim text, in the same row as
    the name and the DEMO SPEED badge.
  - It is there in both play modes, because ageing has always happened in
    both, and it updates itself as the dog gets older.
  - New in Needs: an "aged" signal, which fires when the age as it is said
    on screen changes rather than every frame. 855 checks pass, up from
    848.

Why
  - The dog has aged since v1.13 and the number was already written down,
    but the only way to see it was Mode - Scoreboard, which is a screen
    about dogs that have died. Nobody opens that to check on a dog that is
    fine, so the owner asked for the age where it can simply be read.

How
  - One wording, in one place. UiBits.age_line joins LifeModel.format_age
    to the life stage, and both the header and the Scoreboard's entry for
    the living dog now go through it, so the two cannot drift apart. The
    Scoreboard previously built that line itself.
  - The label is driven by the new signal, not polled per frame, in the
    same way the need bars are driven by need_changed. Needs keeps the last
    age wording it announced - the same trick _shown uses for the
    percentages - so the signal fires on a whole new hour, a whole new day
    or a new stage, and nothing else. The age itself creeps up every frame;
    what is written on screen does not.
  - Built in code from UiBits.dim rather than placed in Main.tscn, so it is
    a dim line like every other quiet piece of text in the app. HeaderRow
    gained a unique name so main.gd can reach it; that is the whole of the
    scene change.
  - A scenario in tests/test_integration.gd boots the real Main scene with
    a seven-day-old save and checks the label is on screen, sits in the
    header, and says exactly what the life model says - then starts a new
    dog and checks it reads as a newborn Puppy rather than keeping the age
    of the one before.

v1.31 - Add Art: the import wizard writes the pet.json for you
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - A new import wizard, reached from Assets - Add Art in the menu bar and
    from Add art in Settings - Assets. It takes loose files - a .glb, a
    .gltf with its companion files, one or more PNGs - works out what they
    could be, asks only what cannot be measured, then writes the pet.json
    or scene.json itself and copies everything into assets/.
  - A 3D model is asked two things: how big the dog is (small / normal /
    big) and which way it faces, dragged on a slider against a live preview
    of the model in the window. Nothing else - the model is measured and
    scaled as it always was, so its own scale never mattered and now nobody
    has to know that.
  - Pictures are listed one per row with the job each does. For a dog that
    is the state it belongs to - idle, walk, happy and the rest - with the
    frame count already read off the sheet's width and offered for
    correcting. For a room it is backdrop, wall tile, floor tile, or a thing
    standing in the room, placed in words: on the left, near the front,
    medium, the food bowl.
  - A room can have no pictures at all. The generated backgrounds the app
    has always been able to paint - an indoor room, a yard, a park with a
    path - are offered as "drawn by DogPet", so a couple of props make a
    whole room. Only the walk slot gets a background that scrolls.
  - A .zip that is already a pack still goes straight to PackArchive, so
    there is one button for everything now instead of two. Import pack is
    gone from Settings; Add art replaces it.
  - Credit is asked for every time, with the reason said plainly, and a pack
    about to be installed without one is warned about on the same screen.
  - New: scripts/pack_builder.gd, scripts/pack_install.gd,
    scripts/import_wizard.gd, scripts/import_form.gd,
    scripts/import_preview.gd, scenes/ImportWizard.tscn,
    tests/test_builder.gd. 848 checks pass, up from 760.

Why
  - The old Import pack only ever accepted a .zip that already held a
    pet.json. Anyone who had just downloaded a model from Poly Pizza or
    drawn a dog in Aseprite had to make a folder, copy somebody else's
    pet.json, work out what target_height and rotation_y_degrees and frames
    meant, and get the JSON syntactically right, with a silent pet that
    does not appear as the only feedback. That is the one part of DogPet
    that asked the owner to know how DogPet works.
  - Everything the app needed from that file except two numbers was already
    derivable: the model's size is measured from its geometry, a sheet's
    frame size from its header, the frame count from its proportions, the
    file names from the files. The two that are not - which way a model
    faces, and which strip is the walk - are now a slider against a preview
    and a dropdown.

How
  - The thinking is in two scripts with no nodes in them. PackBuilder says
    what a set of files could become, suggests the name, the folder name,
    the file names and the frame count, and builds the manifest.
    PackInstall checks the answers, decides what lands where under what
    name, and copies it. Both are driven by tests/test_builder.gd: real
    PNGs in, a real pack folder out, read back with PetPack.load_from_dir
    and ScenePack.load_from_dir, because a wizard that writes a manifest
    the app cannot load has helped nobody.
  - The questions are controls, and the answers are read off them, which is
    a seam no pure test can see: the builder can be perfectly correct while
    the form hands it the wrong field. So the wizard is also driven end to
    end by a scenario in tests/test_integration.gd, in the real Main scene
    with the real autoloads - a sprite pet from two PNGs, a drawn room, and
    the shipped pug installed as a second 3D pet - with assets/ pointed at
    a sandbox so nothing lands in the project folder.
  - Models and their companion files keep their own file names, because a
    .gltf refers to its .bin and its textures by name and renaming them
    would break the model invisibly. Pictures are renamed to the characters
    an id may hold, since the wizard writes the references itself. Two
    files of the same name from different folders do not overwrite each
    other; the second becomes walk_2.png.
  - A name that reduces to a Windows device name - CON.png becomes con.png,
    which is still the console - is prefixed rather than kept. That is
    PackArchive's rule for zip entries applied to chosen files, through its
    clean_id, which stopped being private for the purpose.
  - PackTransfer.pick, confirm and back_up are public now, and pick takes a
    dialog mode and its filters, so the wizard asks for loose art through
    the same Windows picker and reuses the same ask-then-back-up before
    replacing anything. import_pack went with the button that called it.
  - Two things the wizard found rather than added. A room made only of
    props had nowhere to go, which is what turned the generated backgrounds
    into an offer. And a missing credit cannot simply be allowed: PetPack
    counts one as a problem, so the wizard says so while the field is still
    on screen instead of letting it be discovered in the pack list.

v1.30 - An outside optimisation pass: 228 lines out, same behaviour
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - 228 lines of code out of 7,354, with no change in behaviour. 760 checks
    pass before and after. Thirteen commits, one per finding, each named
    optimize(F-0NN) so any one of them can be reverted on its own.
  - The big one: all eight test suites carried a byte-identical copy of the
    counters, _ok, _begin, _close and the closing tally. They now extend
    tests/suite.gd. 160 lines.
  - Six unreferenced symbols deleted: AssetLibrary.credit_lines,
    SceneView.prop_screen_position, Settings.starve_grace_seconds,
    PetPack.sprite_path, PetAnimator.current_pose and
    ZipSizes.EOCD_SIGNATURE. 47 lines.
  - The scoreboard and the credits list now build their labels through
    UiBits, which is what ui_bits.gd's own header said should happen when it
    was written. 24 lines.
  - One duration formatter with a compact flag, replacing UiBits.duration
    and AppMenu._short, which were the same function with different unit
    strings. One panel-frame builder replacing six hand-assembled copies.
    PackLinks.web_url matches its scheme once instead of twice.
  - redteam/* and .optimization/* are excluded from the export. This one is
    not about size.

Why
  - The export filter covered tests/* but not redteam/*, which has no
    .gdignore - so Godot imported its sixteen probe scripts and packed them
    into the shipped exe, including the two that demonstrate code execution.
    Worth finding on its own.
  - Everything else was ordinary tidying, with the test harness the one
    genuinely large duplication in the project.

How
  - Branch optimize/2026-10-31 off a clean tree, a check script re-run after
    every single change, and a full read of all 63 in-scope files before
    anything was touched. Dead code first, so nothing was refactored that
    was about to be deleted.
  - Every deletion was proven unreferenced across scripts, tests, scenes,
    tools and assets first. The project has no dynamic dispatch anywhere -
    no call(), no has_method(), no string-keyed lookup - so those reference
    counts can be trusted rather than guessed at.
  - The suites' printed output is a contract: build.ps1 decides whether a
    release proceeds by grepping it. All eight were diffed against their
    old output. The only differences are line numbers inside Godot's own
    backtraces for the parse errors two suites provoke deliberately, which
    move because lines above them went.
  - Two fixes were applied, measured and reverted. Sharing the dialog
    boilerplate came out four lines LONGER and turned named property
    assignments into five positional arguments. Turning ActionPlan's match
    statements into lookup tables saved two lines and read worse. Both were
    green at 760/760 first; they worked, they just were not worth keeping.
  - Three were skipped with reasons recorded, including a base class for
    PetPack and ScenePack: real structural gain, zero lines, because what
    comes out reappears as a new file with its own header comment.
  - That last point is the honest finding about this code. It is already at
    the point where an abstraction costs more lines than the duplication it
    removes, because every new construct carries an explanatory comment by
    convention - fifteen to twenty lines before any logic. A helper
    replacing six copies netted three lines. Stripping those comments to
    make the number look better was never on the table; they say WHY.
  - The full write-up, the file-by-file ledger and the check scripts are in
    .optimization/, which is excluded from git rather than committed.

v1.29 - A closed-catalogue edition, and what closing it actually buys
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added SPECIFICATION-BUNDLED.md: the same tool-agnostic specification, in a
    variant where every pet, background and room ships inside the application
    and the user picks from a fixed catalogue. Import and export are gone
    entirely, and so is the loose content folder.

Why
  - Asked for as a separate document rather than a change to the open one,
    which is right: they are two products. One sells "swap in your own dog",
    the other sells a curated set and a much smaller attack surface. Keeping
    both means the choice can be made with the consequences written down.

How
  - Not a strip-out. Closing the content system ripples: the content service

v1.24 - The last nine audit findings, from frozen windows to lost saves
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - RT-05. A room whose tiles are tiny is drawn at a larger whole-number
    scale instead of one draw call per screen pixel. SceneView has its own
    ceiling for a tile whose size could not be read beforehand.
  - RT-06. Saving no longer deletes the previous good save to make room for
    the new one. The backup is taken by copying, the new file goes into
    place last, and a save that could not refresh its backup says so.
    history.json gets the same treatment.
  - RT-07. An absence longer than thirty days is treated as a broken clock:
    the needs still drain in full, but the dog does not age, fall ill or
    starve over a number the app does not believe. Said on the away card.
  - RT-08. Replacing a pack on import now empties the folder first, after
    the backup has been confirmed. "Replace" used to mean "write over".
  - RT-09. Numbers read back from a save or settings file have to be finite,
    and the four functions that turn a number into words say "unknown"
    rather than printing rubbish.
  - RT-10. A PNG declaring a side too big to express is refused rather than
    mistaken for "size unknown". PetPack's duplicate header reader is gone.
  - RT-11. The log rotates while the app is running, not only at startup.
  - RT-12. The wscript notification helper is written to the owner's own
    user data folder under an unpredictable name, not to saves/notify.vbs.
  - RT-13. Zip entries named after Windows devices - CON, NUL, COM1 - are
    refused alongside the ".." paths that already were.

Why
  - A 1x1 pixel tile asked for 3.7 million draw calls a frame on a maximised
    window. The window stops responding, and the room is remembered in
    settings.json, so restarting goes straight back into it.
  - write_atomic removed the old backup, then moved the only good save aside,
    then deleted it outright if the move failed - and still reported success.
    On Windows that move fails whenever anything holds the file open:
    antivirus, a sync client, Explorer's preview pane. The next corrupt write
    then had nothing to fall back on, which is the one thing the file exists
    to prevent. Proven by making pet.json.bak unusable: the dog was gone.
  - elapsed_between guarded a clock that moved backwards but not forwards,
    which is the likelier fault - a dead CMOS battery reads 2099, not 1970.
    A save a year old meant a year of starvation credited during the
    catch-up, so the dog was dead before the window opened.
  - Importing a pack over an existing one left behind every file the new one
    did not happen to overwrite: a pet.json with no walk strip listed, and
    the old walk.png still sitting beside it.
  - A save can hold 1e999. JSON parses it as inf and stringify writes it back
    as 1e99999, so once an infinity is in a save file it stays there - and an
    infinite age printed "-9223372036854775808 days old" on the scoreboard,
    because int() of an infinity is undefined.
  - Vector2i holds 32-bit components, so a PNG claiming 4294967295 wide
    wrapped to -1 and read as "size unknown", which is never too large. The
    one file the guard existed to refuse went straight to the decoder.
  - The log's 1 MB ceiling was only ever applied at launch, and this app is
    meant to be left in the tray for days.
  - saves/ sits beside the exe so the app stays portable. That is right for a
    save file and wrong for a script the app then executes: unzip DogPet
    somewhere several accounts can reach and anyone who can write there can
    replace notify.vbs and have it run as the owner.
  - Windows resolves CON and COM1 to devices whatever folder they are in.
    Nothing was known to go wrong, but a serial port can block when opened.

How
  - RT-07 took two goes. Capping the elapsed time was not enough on its own:
    thirty days of starvation is far past any grace period, so a clock that
    jumped a year still killed the dog, and the integration test said so. No
    cap can work, because stopping that needs a cap below one grace period
    while leaving real absences alone needs one far above it. So the two
    halves of a catch-up are now treated by how recoverable they are - a
    maxed bar is one click from fixed, a death is not - and the irreversible
    half is skipped when the clock cannot be believed. This is the one change
    here that alters Real play: a dog genuinely left for forty days now
    survives to be looked at. Reverting it is deleting one if in Needs.
  - Godot's rename_absolute does overwrite an existing file on Windows,
    contrary to the comment in SaveFile that justified the old destructive
    order. Checked before relying on it. So the new save can go into place
    last and nothing good is destroyed first.
  - Three fixes moved while being written, because the code they belonged in
    could not be tested: a script that names an autoload cannot be compiled
    by a --script run at all, which is what ImageLimits and CareSettings
    already say. PackLinks and ZipSizes exist for that reason, and the folder
    delete went to PackArchive.empty_folder rather than PackTransfer.
  - Two defects introduced and caught by running the probes rather than by
    reading the diff: raw_to_base64 complains at an empty buffer, and
    get_tree() is null in an autoload before it is in the tree, so the vbs
    helper is swept up at the start of the next notification instead of on a
    timer.
  - Checked that none of it refuses real work: all three shipped packs still
    export and re-import, the shipped rooms come through with their tile
    scales untouched, a genuinely starving dog still dies on time, real
    toasts still appear with the tray icon on them, and a name with accents
    or Japanese in it reaches PowerShell with every code point intact.
  - 760 checks pass, 0 fail, up from 637 before the audit. 123 of those are
    new regression guards across all eight suites. The full write-up is in
    redteam/, which is not committed: it holds working proofs of concept and
    quotes absolute paths.

v1.23 - A zip that cannot eat all the memory, and a grace period that holds
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - RT-03. Import pack now reads how big a zip says it is before reading any
    of it, and refuses one that claims more than it will handle: 256 KB for
    a pet.json or scene.json, 64 MB for any one file, 256 MB for the pack,
    512 files. The new scripts/zip_sizes.gd reads those figures out of the
    zip's own index without decompressing anything.
  - RT-04. The Real play numbers in settings.json now have floors under
    them, the same way the reminder gap and the action length already did.
    A starve grace of zero no longer means the dog dies this instant.

Why
  - A zip's compression ratio has no ceiling. A 597 KB file holding a
    pet.json of 600 MB of spaces took peak memory to 3,024 MB and froze the
    app for four and a half seconds - and it was accepted, because the JSON
    parser skips the padding. At 4 GB of padding the zip is still only about
    4 MB and the app is simply gone. Worse, this happened the moment the
    file was chosen in the dialog, before the "Replace?" confirmation.
  - A starve grace of zero killed a perfectly fed dog on the first frame:
    starving_seconds is set to zero for a dog that is not hungry, and
    "0 >= 0" is true. Starting another one from the death card did it again,
    writing a record into history.json each time. Real play became
    unplayable with nothing on screen to say why. settings.json is meant to
    be edited by hand, so it has to survive being edited badly.

How
  - ZIPReader in Godot 4.7.2 will not say how big an entry is - it has only
    open, close, get_files, read_file, file_exists and get_compression_level
    (CLAUDE.md's note about get_file_info() being there since 4.4 is wrong).
    So zip_sizes.gd walks the zip's central directory with FileAccess.
  - That figure is worth trusting for this one purpose, even though a zip's
    metadata is otherwise only a claim: read_file resizes its buffer to
    exactly the declared size and reads at most that many bytes, so the
    declaration IS the allocation. A zip that under-declares only gets a
    truncated read. Checking the declaration therefore really does bound the
    memory.
  - Anything that cannot be measured confidently is refused rather than read
    hopefully: no index, a cut-short index, or zip64 - which exists for
    archives past 4 GB or 65535 files, and a pet is neither.
  - The limits are hard-coded rather than put in settings.json like every
    other number here. A limit the owner can edit is a limit an unpacked
    pack could edit, and then it is not a limit.
  - The floors are MIN_STARVE_GRACE_INTERVALS, MIN_AGE_DAY_INTERVALS (zero
    divides by zero and makes the dog's age infinite) and
    MIN_VET_VISIT_HEALS (a vet that heals nothing leaves an ill dog with no
    way back). sickness_rise_intervals and illness_mean_intervals are
    deliberately left alone: every use of them already guards against zero,
    where they simply mean "an illness never gets worse" and "the dog never
    falls ill". A floor there would introduce a death rather than prevent
    one, and would take away a setting somebody might be using on purpose.
  - LifeModel.advance also now refuses to treat a grace of zero or less as
    an instruction to kill the dog, whatever it is handed. The floors mean
    it should never see one; it is the one model here where being wrong
    costs somebody their pet, and it should not depend on its caller.
  - Checked that none of this refuses real work. All three shipped packs
    export and re-import cleanly - the biggest, pug_3d, is 1.6 MB against a
    64 MB ceiling - and a genuinely starving dog still dies after exactly
    one care interval. The bomb now costs inspect 1 KB and one millisecond,
    where it used to cost about 3 GB and four and a half seconds.
  - 686 checks pass, 0 fail, up from 654. 32 of the new ones guard these two
    fixes: _test_zip_sizes in test_packs.gd and _test_broken_tuning in
    test_life.gd.

v1.22 - Two ways a file could run code, both closed
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - RT-01. The dog's name is no longer part of the PowerShell script that
    raises a Windows notification. The name, the message and the icon path
    now travel as base64 and the toast's XML is built through the DOM, so
    the script DogPet runs is a fixed piece of text with no room in it for
    anything a dog might be called.
  - RT-02. A pack's licence and source links are only clickable when they
    are plain http:// or https:// addresses. Anything else is still shown -
    it has to be, because showing the licence URI is part of the credit -
    but as text that does nothing when clicked. The rule is the new
    scripts/pack_links.gd, with 17 checks in tests/test_packs.gd.

Why
  - Both were a file being trusted the way typed-in text is trusted, and
    both ended in code running.
  - The name went into an @"..."@ here-string, which PowerShell expands, and
    the substitution happened in GDScript - so the name was in the script's
    own source before PowerShell had read it. A dog called $(...) had its
    name run as a command. The XML escaping was right as far as it went; the
    trouble was that two parsers read that text one after the other and it
    only ever satisfied the second. Proven by setting the name to a
    Set-Content call and watching the file appear.
  - license_url went from pet.json straight to OS.shell_open, which is
    ShellExecute: it will open a document, launch an exe, find a bare name
    on the PATH, or hand the string to any protocol handler. A pack could
    ship a link labelled "CC-BY 3.0" that ran a program from a network
    share. Proven by pointing it at a .cmd file and clicking it.
  - Neither needed anything unusual of the owner: typing a name, or
    clicking what looks like a licence link on a pack someone sent them.
    assets/ being loose and shareable is the best thing about this app and
    it is also its whole attack surface.

How
  - notifier.gd: _xml_escape is gone - CreateTextNode and SetAttribute do
    that job in the DOM, which is the layer that knows what needs escaping.
    _base64 replaces it as the one way a value may enter the script, and
    answers an empty string directly, because raw_to_base64 complains at an
    empty buffer and a blank body or a missing icon are both normal.
    _file_uri no longer percent-encodes spaces; nothing needs it to now.
  - credits_list.gd: asks PackLinks.web_url first, and falls back to a plain
    dimmed label. PackLinks is reached by preload rather than by its
    class_name, the way CareSettings reaches NeedsModel, so a headless build
    works without waiting for the editor to rescan.
  - PackLinks allows two schemes rather than refusing a list of dangerous
    ones: which schemes are dangerous depends on what is installed on the
    machine, and it cannot know that. It also refuses a bare "https://" and
    anything holding whitespace or a control character.
  - Checked that none of this broke the point of the features. A real toast
    still appears, with the tray icon on it, and the shipped pug's CC-BY
    licence and Poly Pizza source links both still work. A name with
    accents or Japanese in it arrives in PowerShell with every code point
    intact - the first printout suggested otherwise, which turned out to be
    the test's own console codepage rather than the fix.
  - 654 checks pass, 0 fail, up from 637. The project opens with no script
    errors. Found by an outside audit of the whole of scripts/; its report
    and the eleven findings still open are in redteam/, uncommitted.

v1.11 - A build that stops growing every time it runs

------------------------------------------------------------
v1.5 - Phase 2: pets, loaded from assets at runtime
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added the AssetLibrary autoload: scans assets/pets/ and offers every
    folder holding a readable pet.json.
  - Added PetPack, PetLoader, PetPose, PetMotion, PetAnimator and PetView,
    plus scenes/PetView.tscn.
  - The debug screen now shows the dog instead of a placeholder, and has a
    pet picker and a state picker beside it.
  - Added res://builtin/pets/pug_3d/, the fallback pack packed into the exe.
  - Added tests/test_pets.gd: 93 checks. 222 across both suites.

Why
  - Phase 2 of the build plan. All three shipped packs load: pug_3d,
    pawsome_3d and pug_pixel.
  - Neither 3D pack has a rig or a single animation clip - they are static
    meshes - so the life has to come from moving the whole dog about. That
    makes the procedural animation the main path, not an edge case, and it
    turns out to work just as well on a 2D sprite.

How
  - Art in assets/ is never imported by Godot, so a .glb there is parsed at
    runtime with GLTFDocument and a PNG with Image.load_from_file. The
    built-in copy inside res://builtin/ IS imported, so it loads with
    load(). PetLoader picks by whether the folder starts with res://.
  - Models are measured and scaled to one height, with their feet brought
    to the floor and centred left to right. The pug and pawsome are about
    3x apart as modelled and come out the same size.
  - Pixel art is scaled by whole numbers only, one scale for the whole
    pack, taken from its tallest frame. The pixel pug's idle frames are 43
    tall and its walk frames are 32, so scaling each to fit on its own
    would have made the dog change size the moment it started walking.
  - A 3D pet is rendered in a SubViewport with a transparent background, so
    it can stand in front of a flat 2D room in phase 3. Both kinds get a
    soft blob shadow, which is what sells the dog as standing on a floor.
  - The pose maths is pure functions of state and time, so a state can be
    scrubbed to any moment and the tests can check it without a window.
    State changes blend over a third of a second rather than snapping.

Fixed
  - PetView.tscn declared its script as an external resource but never
    assigned it to the root node, so the instance was a bare Control. The
    app came up with no dog and no error at all.
  - The 2D walk sprite came out squashed. Walking turns the dog side on
    with a yaw, and a sprite showed that as a horizontal squeeze - but a
    walk strip is already drawn side on. The squeeze is now only used when
    the pack has no art of its own for that state.
  - The pet picker kept showing the old name when the pet was changed by
    anything other than the picker itself.

Known
  - The care buttons still apply their effects instantly. The rooms, the
    transitions and the action timings are phase 3.
  - Sleep is implemented and can be picked from the state list, but nothing
    enters it on its own yet.
  - The need icons in assets/ui/ are still not drawn; that is phase 5.

v1.4 - Phase 1: skeleton, needs, saves and the debug screen
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added project.godot: Compatibility renderer, 540x760 window with a
    420x600 minimum, canvas_items stretch, nearest-neighbour texture
    filtering, and four autoloads.
  - Added the autoloads Paths, Log, Settings and Needs.
  - Added the pure logic classes NeedsModel, CareSettings and SaveFile.
  - Added Main.tscn, NeedsBar.tscn and AwayCard.tscn with their scripts:
    the three bars, their countdowns, the care-interval picker, the Demo
    Speed toggle, instant care buttons and the "While you were away" card.
  - Added tests/test_needs.gd: 129 checks, all passing.
  - Bumped the version to 1.4 and ticked phase 1 in README.md.

Why
  - Phase 1 of the build plan: the plumbing under the pet, before there is
    a pet. Needs that tick in real time, save to disk, and catch up on the
    time the app was closed.
  - The maths is split away from the nodes so it can be tested headlessly,
    which is what section 6.6 asks for. Settings got the same treatment:
    the data and lookups live in CareSettings, apart from the autoload,
    because a --script run cannot reach an autoload at all.

How
  - Drain is linear at 100 / interval per second, clamped at the bad end.
    The dog never dies or runs away; it stays sad until cared for.
  - Saves are written to pet.json.tmp and moved into place, keeping the
    file they replace as pet.json.bak. A pet.json that will not parse
    falls back to the backup.
  - Catch-up always uses the real care setting, never Demo Speed, so a
    demo session cannot max every bar the moment the app reopens.
  - Paths tests that it can really write a file in saves/, not just that
    the folder exists, and falls back to the Windows user data folder if
    not, with a notice on screen saying where saves went.

Fixed
  - A need that drained to exactly 70% of its interval read as Fine
    rather than Getting low. 100/10800*7560 is 69.99999, so it sat a hair
    under the threshold and its warning would never have fired. The
    warning check now has the floating-point slack the critical check
    already had. Caught by the tests.
  - The away card sat on the default panel, which is translucent, so the
    screen behind showed through its own text.
  - A progress bar fill with rounded corners still draws a sliver at
    value 0, so an empty bar looked very slightly full.
  - The Demo Speed toggle only knew about clicks on itself, so a change
    made anywhere else left it showing the old state.

Known
  - The care buttons apply their effects instantly. The animations, the
    rooms and the action timings arrive in phase 3.
  - The need icons from assets/ui/ are not drawn yet; the asset loader is
    phase 2. The bars use text labels in the meantime.
