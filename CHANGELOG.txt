v1.17 - Real play fixes: the death nobody was told about
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - A dog that died while DogPet was closed now gets its death card when
    the window opens. Before, the app came up with everything frozen, no
    card and no way to start another dog: the save was unplayable for good,
    because nothing ever told the owner what had happened.
  - The offline catch-up no longer credits a dog with starving for the
    whole time the app was shut. Only the part of the absence after hunger
    actually reached the bad end counts towards the grace period.
  - Feed, Play, Walk and Take To Vet do nothing at all once the dog has
    died, and an action that was still running when it died no longer
    unlocks the buttons as it finishes or tops up the needs afterwards.
  - Starting a new dog announces the reset, so its bars no longer open
    reading "Needs care now" in red, inherited from the dog before it.
  - A need that was already at Warning or Critical when the app opened is
    now known about: critical ones get their reminders scheduled, where
    before they were never mentioned again.
  - The bars say "no longer counting" rather than promising care in two
    hours' time for a dog that has died.
  - The "While you were away" card now says the dog died, where it used to
    report it as merely starving.
  - The Real Pet confirmation dialog's text had a real line break sitting
    inside an ordinary quoted string. Godot 4.7 accepts that, but most
    GDScript will not, so the break is now written as \n\n. The dialog
    reads exactly as before.
  - Added 10 checks to tests/test_needs.gd and 8 to tests/test_life.gd:
    560 across seven suites.

Why
  - All of it came out of a review of Real play, and the first item is the
    one that mattered: Needs does its offline catch-up in its own _ready,
    which runs before the first scene exists. Anything it announced by
    signal at that moment was announced to an empty room. The away card was
    already written around that, and the death was not.
  - The starvation arithmetic was the other one worth having. A dog fed
    right before the app closed and reopened three hours and one minute
    later on Moderate was credited with three hours and a minute of
    starvation against a three-hour grace, and died on the spot, when the
    truth was that it had been starving for a minute.

How
  - Needs no longer emits pet_died or fell_ill while catching up, where
    there is nobody to hear it. Main asks life.alive in its own _ready
    instead, which also covers reopening a save that was already dead.
  - NeedsModel.seconds_at_critical says how much of a stretch of draining a
    need spent at its bad end. Needs splits the absence on that figure and
    advances the life model twice: once well fed, once starving. The split
    is pure arithmetic, so both tests drive it directly.
  - Main's _set_actions_enabled now goes through life.alive, so every path
    that re-enables the buttons is covered by one check rather than three.

v1.16 - Phase 9: the scoreboard of past pets
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added PetHistory, which keeps saves/history.json, and the Scoreboard
    panel, reached from Mode > Scoreboard.
  - Every dog that dies is written into the record with its name, birth
    date, death date, age and what took it.
  - Added tests/test_history.gd: 37 checks. 542 across seven suites.

Why
  - The last of the three sets of changes the owner asked for.

How
  - The record is kept apart from pet.json because it outlives every pet in
    it: starting a new dog wipes the save, and must not wipe the record of
    the one before.
  - A dog is written in at the moment it dies rather than when the owner
    gets round to starting another, and only there, so reloading a save of
    an already-dead dog cannot record it twice - LifeModel.advance returns
    early once alive is false.
  - The scoreboard lists the dog you have now first, marked as living,
    because a board that only lists the dead is a strange thing to open
    when your dog is fine.
  - Each past dog shows both its age in dog-days and how long it really
    lived. The two differ a great deal: a dog on Demo speed ages a year in
    an afternoon.
  - It is reachable in Casual as well, where it still shows the dog you
    have and any from an earlier spell of Real play.
  - The record is capped at 200 dogs, oldest off the end first, so the file
    cannot grow forever.

Fixed
  - The summary line read "3 dogs remembered.  the oldest was ..." - full
    stops between fragments rather than one sentence.

Fixed in the build script
  - It ran five of the seven test suites. The list was written out by hand
    and quietly stopped covering test_life and test_history when they were
    added, so a failure in either would not have stopped a release - and
    those are the two suites about the dog dying. It finds the suites in
    tests/ for itself now.

That is all three sets of changes done.

v1.15 - A nurse in the vet clinic
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added scenes/vet/vet_nurse.png and stood her behind the exam table.

Why
  - The owner asked whether the nurse from the Mini Clinic Pixel pack
    could be shrunk down and put in the vet scene. She could.

How
  - The pack draws her at ten times her native size: one figure is 1820 by
    5580 in the file, which is 182 by 558 of real pixels.
  - She is taken down to 20 by 60 and shown at the props' scale of 3, so
    she lands at 60 by 180 on screen with the same three-pixel blocks as
    the rest of the room's furniture. A finer scale was tried and looked
    wrong beside it: her pixels were smaller than the exam table's.
  - 80px tall was tried first and reached the ceiling. Real play adds a
    fourth bar, which leaves the room about 240px tall rather than 310, so
    anything sized against a Casual room is too big for a Real one.
  - Her y puts her just behind the exam table. Props are drawn back to
    front by y, so a smaller number than the table's leaves her attending
    it rather than standing in front of it.

Known
  - The clinic is now three art styles at once: an anime-ish nurse,
    isometric furniture and a 3D dog. It reads well enough, but it is a
    mixture, and worth a look before deciding it stays.

v1.13 - Phase 8: Casual and Real Pet, ageing, illness and death
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added a Mode menu: Casual and Real Pet.
  - Added LifeModel: ageing, illness, starvation and death, with no nodes
    in it, and tests/test_life.gd with 77 checks.
  - Real play adds a fourth Sickness bar and a Take To Vet button, neither
    of which appears in Casual.
  - Added the vet clinic room and the "vet" scene role.
  - Added a generated background style, "room": a plain indoor wall,
    skirting band and floor, drawn in code from colours.
  - Added DeathCard, which is how a Real play game ends and restarts.
  - 505 checks passing across six suites.

Why
  - The second of three sets of changes the owner asked for.
  - Casual is the game as it was: the dog cannot die, whatever happens.

How
  - Everything in Real play is counted in hunger intervals rather than
    minutes, so it all scales with the care setting. On Demo an illness
    runs its course in a minute; on Long it takes most of a day.
  - A starving dog gets one further care interval before it dies, and that
    can happen during the offline catch-up. The owner chose this knowing
    it means a long enough absence is fatal.
  - Old age is never fatal, also by choice. The dog ages through Puppy,
    Young, Adult and Senior at two hunger intervals to the dog-day, in
    both modes, and that is all ageing does.
  - Illness arrives on an average wait rather than a per-tick chance, so a
    week away is no more likely to produce an illness than a week spent
    watching - and no less.
  - One trip to the vet takes 60 off a bar that runs to 100, so a dog left
    until it is nearly gone has to be taken back.
  - Switching to Real play asks first, since it is the choice that lets the
    dog die. Switching back to Casual does not, and clears any illness.
  - The vet art is the ThirdAngle Veterinary Clinic Starter, credited in
    assets/CREDITS.txt. Only its props are used: the pack's walls and
    floors are isometric diamonds that cannot sit in DogPet's flat
    side-on rooms, so the clinic's room is drawn in code instead. The
    sprites were trimmed of their transparent margins, which the licence
    allows, because DogPet anchors a prop by the bottom of its image and
    the margin would have left every one of them hovering.

Fixed
  - An ill dog read as "doing just fine" and behaved like it. Illness and
    death now come first in both the mood and the line under the dog.

Known
  - The Sickness bar has no icon; assets/ui has none for it.
  - Six scripts are now a little over the 300-line guide, needs.gd and
    scene_view.gd among them.
  - The Scoreboard item in the Mode menu says it is still to come.

Still to come
  - The scoreboard of past pets.

v1.12 - Phase 7: a menu bar, and instructions inside the app
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added a menu bar across the top of the window, with four menus:
      Pet      Change Name..., Settings...
      Care     Frequent / Moderate / Long, and Demo Speed
      Assets   Open Assets Folder, Reload Assets, How to Change Pets or
               Scenes...
      Window   Minimise to Tray, Quit DogPet
  - Added a rename dialog, reached from Pet > Change Name.
  - Added HelpPanel: step-by-step instructions for changing the art, with
    buttons to open the assets folder and reload at the bottom.
  - Added Paths.open_in_file_manager.

Why
  - The first of three sets of changes the owner asked for. Everything in
    the menus was already reachable in Settings or on the tray icon, but a
    menu bar is where people look first, and it puts the things done most
    often one click away instead of two.
  - The instructions exist because assets/README.txt sits next to the exe,
    which is exactly where somebody who needs the help will not think to
    look.

How
  - The menus are built in code, because most of them describe the state of
    something - which care speed is chosen, whether Demo is on, whether
    there is a tray to minimise into. They are refreshed each time a menu
    opens rather than kept in step by hand.
  - The care menu shows each setting's timings beside its name, so the
    choice can be made without going to look them up.
  - Main gained a vertical shell so the menu bar can sit flush against the
    top of the window while everything else keeps its margins.
  - Opening a folder hands it to the system rather than naming Explorer, so
    a replacement file manager gets it if one is installed. Godot holds
    paths with forward slashes, which Explorer is unreliable about, so they
    are turned back into backslashes on the way out. The folder is made if
    it is missing, since assets/ genuinely can be - the app falls back to
    its built-in art and carries on - and opening nothing would be a
    puzzling way to find that out.

Still to come
  - Casual and Real Pet modes, ageing, sickness and the vet.
  - The scoreboard of past pets.

v1.11 - A build that stops growing every time it runs
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - build/ and docs/ now carry a .gdignore, so Godot leaves them alone.
  - tools/build.ps1 prepares Godot's caches before running the tests, not
    after.

Why
  - Every build came out several megabytes bigger than the one before it:
    112.8 MB, then 118.4, then 127.1, for changes that touched a single
    script. build/ sits inside the project, and the assets staged into it
    have their .gdignore stripped on purpose - the player has no use for
    it - which left Godot free to import every staged copy and pack it into
    the next build. Each build was swallowing the one before it. The exe is
    back to 113 MB and stays there across repeated builds.
  - The v1.9 release was already carrying about 5 MB of the v1.8 staging
    copy, which is why this got its own version rather than being quietly
    folded in.
  - The screenshots in docs/ were being imported and packed into the exe
    too. They are documentation, not art the game uses.

How
  - A .gdignore at the top of build/ is written by the build script itself,
    since build/ is not in version control.
  - The cache step moved ahead of the tests because the tests are scripts
    too: without the class cache Godot cannot resolve the class names they
    use, so a build from a fresh clone failed in test_pets. Found by
    deleting .godot and building, which is what a fresh clone looks like.

v1.10 - Screenshots, and scenery that scales with the window
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added docs/screenshot-home.png, screenshot-walk.png and
    screenshot-settings.png, and put them at the top of README.md in place
    of the placeholder.
  - Drawn scenery - trees, bushes, clouds and path stones - is now sized as
    a fraction of the view height instead of in fixed pixels.

Why
  - Taking the screenshots made an old problem obvious: in the park the dog
    towered over the trees like a giant. The dog has always been sized from
    the view height, while the scenery was drawn at fixed pixel sizes, so
    the two only ever agreed by accident at one particular window size.
    They now scale together, at any size.

How
  - Tree height, bush radius, cloud width, stone size and the spacing of
    each layer are all fractions of the view height. A tree is 34-52% of
    it, which puts it comfortably above the dog rather than below.
  - Tree trunks scale with their own tree rather than staying 8px wide.

v1.9 - Phase 6: shipped - export preset, build script, first release
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added export_presets.cfg: Windows Desktop, x86_64, PCK embedded so the
    exe is a single file, the icon from icons/app_icon.ico, tests left out.
  - Added tools/build.ps1, which runs the tests, exports, copies assets/ in
    beside the exe, writes the player's README.txt and zips the lot.
  - Ran the edge-case pass.
  - First GitHub release.

Why
  - Phase 6 of the build plan, and the end of it. DogPet is a zip you unzip
    and run.

How
  - The exe is about 113 MB because an exported Godot game is the whole
    engine with the project stitched onto it. The zip comes to about 47 MB.
  - assets/ is copied in loose rather than packed into the exe, which is the
    whole point: the art can be changed without rebuilding. The .gdignore
    markers are stripped on the way - they exist only to keep the Godot
    editor out of those folders during development - and _backup is left
    behind.
  - Verified by unzipping into a folder whose path contains a space and
    running it there: it reads the loose assets rather than the built-in
    copies, writes saves next to the exe, and brings up its tray icon.

Fixed
  - The build aborted on its first run. Capturing a native program's stderr
    in Windows PowerShell wraps every line as an error, and with
    ErrorActionPreference Stop that is fatal - so the engine error that
    test_needs provokes deliberately, by feeding a corrupt file to the JSON
    parser, killed the build. The exit code decides now.
  - The export templates check never fired, because Godot creates that
    folder whether or not anything is in it. It looks for a template now.
  - The generated README.txt began with a byte order mark, which shows as a
    stray character in plenty of editors. Windows PowerShell's -Encoding
    UTF8 always writes one, so it is written without one directly.

Checked, with no code changes needed
  - assets/ missing entirely: falls back to the pug, all four rooms and the
    icons from inside the exe, and says so.
  - A read-only app folder: saves relocate to the user data folder with a
    notice saying where they went. Tested by denying write with icacls.
  - pet.json corrupt with a good backup: restored from pet.json.bak.
  - pet.json and backup both corrupt: starts a fresh, cared-for dog.
  - settings.json corrupt: falls back to the defaults.
  - A pet.json that is not JSON, and a pet whose model is missing: skipped
    with a reason, other packs unaffected.
  - A room filling no known slot: skipped with a reason.
  - A room whose tiles and props are missing: still offered, with a drawn
    background and the missing prop left out.

Known
  - The taskbar button stays while minimised to the tray; Godot has no flag
    to drop it.
  - Notifications arrive from "Windows PowerShell", since a portable app
    that installs nothing has no registered name of its own.
  - The exe is unsigned, so Windows SmartScreen will warn the first time.

v1.8 - Phase 5: the settings screen and pack sharing
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added SettingsPanel with all seven sections: Care, Pet, Rooms, Assets,
    Window, Notifications and About.
  - Added PackArchive, PackTransfer and CreditsList.
  - Packs can be exported and imported as .zip files.
  - The main window lost its debug rows and gained a settings button; the
    need bars now show their icons from assets/ui.
  - Added tests/test_packs.gd: 56 checks. 418 across five suites.

Why
  - Phase 5 of the build plan. Everything adjustable now lives in one place
    instead of being scattered across temporary debug controls.
  - Credits are a licence requirement, not decoration. The default pug is
    CC-BY 3.0, so its credit has to stay visible.

How
  - The sections are built in code rather than laid out by hand, because
    almost all of them are lists of whatever happens to be installed: the
    pets found in assets/, the rooms offering themselves for each slot,
    every pack's credit line. None of that is known in advance.
  - Importing checks the zip before unpacking anything, asks before
    replacing, and copies what it is about to overwrite into
    assets/_backup/<id>-<timestamp>/ first.
  - A room is imported under its own folder name rather than its role, so
    importing a second kitchen adds one rather than destroying the first.
  - Exporting writes a CREDITS.txt into the zip, so the credit travels with
    the art rather than being left behind.
  - About shows every installed pack's credit exactly as written, with
    licence and source as clickable links, the pet currently on screen
    first, and the whole of assets/CREDITS.txt underneath.
  - The system file picker is used where Windows offers one, with Godot's
    own dialog as the fallback.

Fixed
  - The settings panel was see-through. Godot's default panel stylebox is
    translucent - the same trap the away card fell into in phase 1.
  - The care interval table collapsed into a wall of vertical letters. The
    helper that makes dimmed labels was turning on word wrap for every one
    of them, and a wrapping label in a narrow table column will happily
    wrap to one character per line. Wrapping is now asked for, not assumed.

Known
  - A zip that names an entry outside its own folder is refused outright,
    whole archive and all, rather than being unpacked with that one entry
    skipped. Refusing it is the safer reading of an archive that should
    never have contained such a path.
  - Pausing notifications is still not remembered across restarts.

v1.7 - Phase 4: tray icon, notifications and banners
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added the Notifier and Tray autoloads, UiPack, NotificationRules and
    the Banner scene.
  - The close button now tucks DogPet away instead of quitting it, with a
    tray icon, a menu, a live tooltip and an alert icon.
  - Windows toast notifications when a need crosses a threshold while the
    owner is not looking, and in-app banners when they are.
  - Added res://builtin/ui/, the fallback icons packed into the exe.
  - The three care buttons now show their icons from assets/ui.
  - Added tests/test_notify.gd: 62 checks. 362 across four suites.

Why
  - Phase 4 of the build plan. A pet you have to keep a window open for is
    not much of a pet.

How
  - Godot cannot raise a Windows toast, so one is raised through
    PowerShell, which ships with every Windows 10 and 11. The script goes
    over base64-encoded, which sidesteps every quoting problem between
    GDScript and PowerShell, and is launched without waiting for it so the
    game never stutters.
  - Notifications show as coming from "Windows PowerShell". Avoiding that
    needs an application registered with Windows, which a portable app that
    installs nothing cannot have. Expected and accepted for v1.
  - If a console window ever flashes, set "notification_launcher" to
    "wscript" in settings.json. Both routes are built; PowerShell launched
    with no console window is the default and showed no flash in testing.
  - While out of sight the frame rate drops to 5 and low processor usage
    mode goes on, so leaving it running costs almost nothing.
  - A need is announced once when it gets worse, never when it gets better,
    and then once per reminder interval while it stays critical.

Fixed
  - Godot's Window.hide() does nothing at all to the root window on
    Windows. Checked from outside the app with IsWindowVisible: the window
    stayed fully up while the app believed it was hidden in the tray.
    Close-to-tray now minimises through DisplayServer, which does work.
    The taskbar button stays visible, which is the one difference from a
    true hide; Godot exposes no flag to drop it. CLAUDE.md section 7.2 has
    been updated so the rest of the build does not trip over this.
  - Restoring the window needed window_move_to_foreground() as well as the
    mode change. Setting the mode back on its own left it minimised while
    Godot reported it restored.
  - The root Window's visible, mode and has_focus() all reported wrongly
    while it sat minimised - visible, windowed and focused. Every
    notification would have gone out as an invisible banner rather than a
    toast, which is the whole point of the feature. The check now asks
    DisplayServer, and the tray's own flag, instead.
  - A regression from v1.6: the screenshot scaffolding used to check this
    work removed the "While you were away" card from Main._ready along with
    itself, so it had not been shown since phase 3. The scaffolding is now
    fenced with explicit markers, and _ready is checked after every removal.

Known
  - The taskbar button stays while minimised to the tray, as above.
  - Notifications are paused for an hour from the tray menu, and that is
    not remembered across restarts - reopening the app is a clear enough
    sign of wanting to be told again.
  - The need icons in assets/ui/ are loaded but not drawn on the bars yet;
    that is phase 5, with the real Settings screen.
  - The tray does not work while the game is embedded in the Godot editor.
    The app says so in its log and the close button quits instead.

v1.6 - Phase 3: rooms, transitions and the care actions
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added ScenePack, SceneView, SceneStage, ActionPlan and ActionRunner.
  - AssetLibrary now scans rooms as well as pets, grouped by the role each
    room declares.
  - The debug screen shows the room behind the dog, with a slot picker and
    a room picker, and the care buttons now run the real sequences.
  - Added res://builtin/scenes/, the fallback rooms packed into the exe.
  - Added tests/test_scenes.gd: 78 checks. 300 across three suites.
  - Settings gained a "rooms" entry remembering the choice per role.

Why
  - Phase 3 of the build plan.
  - Rooms are picked like pets, at the owner's request. This departs from
    section 5.4 of the blueprint, which had one fixed folder per role.
    CLAUDE.md has been updated to match, so the spec and the code do not
    drift apart. It needed no change to the file format, because scene.json
    already carried a "role" field.

How
  - A room declares its role, so a folder can be called anything and two
    rooms can offer themselves for the same slot. A folder with no role
    uses its folder name, which is why the four original folders still work
    untouched.
  - Backgrounds are drawn in _draw rather than built from nodes, which puts
    all three types in one place and makes the scrolling park a matter of
    one offset. The park has three layers at different speeds - clouds
    slowest, trees between, path stones at full speed - which is what gives
    it depth.
  - Props are drawn back to front by their y, so nearer things overlap
    farther ones. x is the middle of a prop and y is its bottom, so props
    sit on the floor rather than floating.
  - Each action locks the buttons, transitions to its room, animates for
    its length, applies its effect, then transitions home and unlocks.
    Both legs use the same transition, so coming back matches going out.
  - The effects land after the animation, not when the button is pressed,
    so the bars move when the dog has actually been fed.

Fixed
  - A 3D dog turning to face left showed the camera its backside. Rotation
    is the only way a model can turn, so the turn now lives in yaw, and
    pose.facing is left to mean "mirror yourself" - which only a sprite can
    actually do.
  - The dog stood on top of the bowl it was eating from. It now stands
    beside a prop, nudged back towards the middle of the room, which also
    keeps it from hanging off the edge when a prop is near one.
  - The window had grown taller than itself: the title was clipped off the
    top and the footer off the bottom. The stale "actions are instant for
    now" label is gone and the footer no longer wraps.

Known
  - The tray icon, notifications and close-to-tray are phase 4.
  - The real Settings screen, the pet and room pickers proper, and importing
    and exporting packs are phase 5. The pickers on the debug screen are
    stand-ins.
  - The need icons in assets/ui/ are still not drawn.

v1.5 - Phase 2: pets, loaded from assets at runtime
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added the AssetLibrary autoload: scans assets/pets/ and offers every
    folder holding a readable pet.json.
  - Added PetPack, PetLoader, PetPose, PetMotion, PetAnimator and PetView,
    plus scenes/PetView.tscn.
  - The debug screen now shows the dog instead of a placeholder, and has a
    pet picker and a state picker beside it.
  - Added res://builtin/pets/pug_3d/, the fallback pack packed into the exe.
  - Added tests/test_pets.gd: 93 checks. 222 across both suites.

Why
  - Phase 2 of the build plan. All three shipped packs load: pug_3d,
    pawsome_3d and pug_pixel.
  - Neither 3D pack has a rig or a single animation clip - they are static
    meshes - so the life has to come from moving the whole dog about. That
    makes the procedural animation the main path, not an edge case, and it
    turns out to work just as well on a 2D sprite.

How
  - Art in assets/ is never imported by Godot, so a .glb there is parsed at
    runtime with GLTFDocument and a PNG with Image.load_from_file. The
    built-in copy inside res://builtin/ IS imported, so it loads with
    load(). PetLoader picks by whether the folder starts with res://.
  - Models are measured and scaled to one height, with their feet brought
    to the floor and centred left to right. The pug and pawsome are about
    3x apart as modelled and come out the same size.
  - Pixel art is scaled by whole numbers only, one scale for the whole
    pack, taken from its tallest frame. The pixel pug's idle frames are 43
    tall and its walk frames are 32, so scaling each to fit on its own
    would have made the dog change size the moment it started walking.
  - A 3D pet is rendered in a SubViewport with a transparent background, so
    it can stand in front of a flat 2D room in phase 3. Both kinds get a
    soft blob shadow, which is what sells the dog as standing on a floor.
  - The pose maths is pure functions of state and time, so a state can be
    scrubbed to any moment and the tests can check it without a window.
    State changes blend over a third of a second rather than snapping.

Fixed
  - PetView.tscn declared its script as an external resource but never
    assigned it to the root node, so the instance was a bare Control. The
    app came up with no dog and no error at all.
  - The 2D walk sprite came out squashed. Walking turns the dog side on
    with a yaw, and a sprite showed that as a horizontal squeeze - but a
    walk strip is already drawn side on. The squeeze is now only used when
    the pack has no art of its own for that state.
  - The pet picker kept showing the old name when the pet was changed by
    anything other than the picker itself.

Known
  - The care buttons still apply their effects instantly. The rooms, the
    transitions and the action timings are phase 3.
  - Sleep is implemented and can be picked from the state list, but nothing
    enters it on its own yet.
  - The need icons in assets/ui/ are still not drawn; that is phase 5.

v1.4 - Phase 1: skeleton, needs, saves and the debug screen
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added project.godot: Compatibility renderer, 540x760 window with a
    420x600 minimum, canvas_items stretch, nearest-neighbour texture
    filtering, and four autoloads.
  - Added the autoloads Paths, Log, Settings and Needs.
  - Added the pure logic classes NeedsModel, CareSettings and SaveFile.
  - Added Main.tscn, NeedsBar.tscn and AwayCard.tscn with their scripts:
    the three bars, their countdowns, the care-interval picker, the Demo
    Speed toggle, instant care buttons and the "While you were away" card.
  - Added tests/test_needs.gd: 129 checks, all passing.
  - Bumped the version to 1.4 and ticked phase 1 in README.md.

Why
  - Phase 1 of the build plan: the plumbing under the pet, before there is
    a pet. Needs that tick in real time, save to disk, and catch up on the
    time the app was closed.
  - The maths is split away from the nodes so it can be tested headlessly,
    which is what section 6.6 asks for. Settings got the same treatment:
    the data and lookups live in CareSettings, apart from the autoload,
    because a --script run cannot reach an autoload at all.

How
  - Drain is linear at 100 / interval per second, clamped at the bad end.
    The dog never dies or runs away; it stays sad until cared for.
  - Saves are written to pet.json.tmp and moved into place, keeping the
    file they replace as pet.json.bak. A pet.json that will not parse
    falls back to the backup.
  - Catch-up always uses the real care setting, never Demo Speed, so a
    demo session cannot max every bar the moment the app reopens.
  - Paths tests that it can really write a file in saves/, not just that
    the folder exists, and falls back to the Windows user data folder if
    not, with a notice on screen saying where saves went.

Fixed
  - A need that drained to exactly 70% of its interval read as Fine
    rather than Getting low. 100/10800*7560 is 69.99999, so it sat a hair
    under the threshold and its warning would never have fired. The
    warning check now has the floating-point slack the critical check
    already had. Caught by the tests.
  - The away card sat on the default panel, which is translucent, so the
    screen behind showed through its own text.
  - A progress bar fill with rounded corners still draws a sliver at
    value 0, so an empty bar looked very slightly full.
  - The Demo Speed toggle only knew about clicks on itself, so a change
    made anywhere else left it showing the old state.

Known
  - The care buttons apply their effects instantly. The animations, the
    rooms and the action timings arrive in phase 3.
  - The need icons from assets/ui/ are not drawn yet; the asset loader is
    phase 2. The bars use text labels in the meantime.

v1.0 - Starter kit and build blueprint
------------------------------------------------------------

What
  - Added CLAUDE.md, the full build spec for Claude Code.
  - Added the assets/ folder with three pet packs (pug_3d, pawsome_3d,
    pug_pixel), four scene packs (home, eat, play, walk) and a UI pack.

    animations, so the spec calls for procedural animation.
  - 2D art copied from Nasha's Cozy Pets Pack and DogPack.
  - Tray icons made from the DogPack pug (sprite 2). The alert icon adds
    a red dot.
  - The exe icon is the same pug scaled 8x with nearest-neighbor.
